In my work, I focus on the intersection of media, technology and society, particularly how information is produced, distributed and consumed by the public. I'm curious about what gets covered and what is left out, and how these choices shape public understanding of their surrounding environment and the world they're living in. More recently, my research has expanded to AI and the questions it raises about how emerging technologies work in practice, who benefits from them and who bears the costs.

Across projects I've managed or partook in, I am interested in questions that are often overlooked, because they are hidden or just difficult to investigate, and in producing research that helps us better understand the technologies and information systems shaping our lives.

I hold a Ph.D. in Journalism and Media Studies from Rutgers University. What complements my research is my background in photography and visual communication, which informs how I examine—and communicate—the relationship between evidence, presentation and public understanding.
